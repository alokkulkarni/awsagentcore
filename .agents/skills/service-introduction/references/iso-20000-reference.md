# ISO/IEC 20000-1:2018 Service Introduction Reference

## Overview

ISO/IEC 20000-1:2018 requires service management systems to manage new and changed services through controlled design, transition, supplier governance, monitoring, and improvement. A Service Introduction Document provides practical evidence that these clauses have been considered and implemented for a specific service.

## Clause Highlights

### Clause 8.5 — Service transition for new or changed services
This clause expects the organization to plan, coordinate, authorize, and verify the transition of new or changed services. The SID maps to this through Document Control, Service Transition Plan, Risk Register, Approvals, and the operational sections that prove readiness.

### Clause 8.2.3 — Service catalogue management
Services must be described consistently and made available through a managed catalogue. The Service Description, Scope, Business Context, and Interfaces sections give the metadata needed for catalogue management and service onboarding.

### Clause 8.3.1 — Service level management
Availability, performance, and recovery expectations must be documented and monitored. The Service Level Objectives and Monitoring & Observability sections are the primary evidence sources.

### Clause 8.4 — Supplier management
Supplier and external dependency relationships must be understood and controlled. The Service Dependencies section explicitly records dependency name, version, owner, and criticality for this purpose.

### Clause 9.1 — Monitoring, measurement, analysis, and evaluation
The organization must measure service performance and evaluate outcomes. The Monitoring & Observability section, SLOs, and value metrics link the service introduction record to measurable operational evidence.

### Clause 8.6 — Service design and transition
Design outputs must be suitable for transition and operation. The full set of SID sections provides a practical design-to-transition artifact for that requirement.

## Practical Audit Guidance

When using the SID in an ISO-aligned environment:
- Link the SID to the formal service catalogue or service portfolio record.
- Reference monitoring dashboards, supplier contracts, and continuity plans where they exist.
- Ensure revision history aligns with the organization’s documented information controls.
- Revalidate the SID when a material architectural, regulatory, supplier, or support-model change occurs.
